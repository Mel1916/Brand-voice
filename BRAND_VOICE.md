# York Jewellers — Brand Voice Guide

York Jewellers (Westfield Penrith, NSW) — bespoke and fine jewellery, engagement rings, repairs and aftercare.

This guide captures how York Jewellers sounds across every channel, sourced from the brand's Klaviyo brand voice profile. Use it as the reference when writing any customer-facing copy.

## Tone

**Warm and Reassuring Expertise**
We combine genuine warmth with clear expertise. We're a trusted partner who understands both jewellery craftsmanship and customer needs — approachable, never cold or jargon-heavy. Customers should feel valued, understood, and confident in their decision.

**Personalized and Collaborative**
We invite customers to co-create their jewellery experience ("Build your own" engagement ring, bespoke design). We're a creative partner who listens and adapts — never a one-sided sales pitch.

**Confident and Transparent Authority**
We speak plainly and confidently about quality, certifications, repairs, resizing, cleaning, and financing. No hedging, no ambiguity — clear information that empowers a decision.

**Inviting and Inspirational**
We connect jewellery to meaningful life moments — "keep what matters close." We use evocative, not sterile or transactional, language that helps customers picture their own story.

## Language Patterns

- **Direct, inclusive pronouns.** Speak as "we" to the customer as "you." Conversational, personal, never third-person distant.
- **Clear, scannable formatting.** Short paragraphs, headings, and bullets — especially for benefits lists and FAQs.
- **Action-oriented, outcome-focused CTAs.** "Build your own," "Get Your Ring Size," "Book an appointment" — CTAs name the next concrete step.
- **Balance emotion with practicality.** Pair emotional language ("celebrate every special occasion") with concrete detail ("certifications to guarantee authenticity," "complimentary resizing") in the same piece.
- **Professional yet approachable vocabulary.** Terms like "bespoke," "certifications," "ultrasonic cleaning" are fine — always used in an explanatory, reassuring context, never left to sound technical on their own.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Written in "we/you," not third person
- [ ] At least one concrete, practical detail (certification, service, timeframe) alongside the emotional hook
- [ ] CTA names a specific next action
- [ ] Scannable — headings/bullets for anything with more than one benefit or step
- [ ] No jargon left unexplained
- [ ] No hedging language ("might," "we think," "possibly") around quality or service claims
